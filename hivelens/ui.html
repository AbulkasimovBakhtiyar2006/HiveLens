System Architecture & Epistemic Verification

HiveLens separates visual segmentation, deterministic biometric measurement, and LLM reasoning so that every statement in a report can be traced back to a verified mask measurement.

01 / Multi-Scale SAM 3.1 Segmentation
Sticky Boards (49 MP) & Comb Frames

On 8064×6048 sticky boards, a Varroa mite is ~30 px wide. Probing showed SAM 3.1 recall rises from 0/3 at 1344 px crops to 3/3 at 300 px crops. HiveLens runs a 600 px grid pass, re-checks unexplained dark spots with 300 px SAM crops, and sends remaining candidates to Muse Spark on 6×6 numbered contact sheets.

02 / Deterministic Biometric Filtering
Physical Plausibility Checks (`hivelens/measure.py`)

Every candidate detection is checked on the raw image via OpenCV contour & ellipse fitting: body area fraction, reddish-brown chrominance relative to the board (`R-G >= 10`), aspect ratio (`<= 1.8`), and oval fill (`>= 0.90`). Wax crumbs and elongated insect fragments are rejected.

03 / Epistemic Agent Gate
Blind Hypotheses Before Measurements (`hivelens/agent.py`)

`get_measurements` is locked until Muse Spark commits to at least two quantitative visual hypotheses via `check_claim`. Each claim returns `verified`, `rejected`, or `unverifiable`. In `submit_report`, any finding that does not cite a verified claim ID is automatically discarded.

04 / Rule-Based Status & Limitations
Non-Overridable Traffic Light (`hivelens/engine.py`)

Thresholds in `config.py` determine `good`, `watch`, or `act`. The language model explains the status and suggests practical checks, but cannot alter the rule verdict or issue a veterinary diagnosis.
