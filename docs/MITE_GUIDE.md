# Honey bee mites: scope and practical checks

Reviewed **5 November 2026**. Broad geographic context, not a live distribution map.
HiveLens evaluates a **Varroa sticky-board counting workflow**. It does not reliably
identify mite species or rule out other infestations from an ordinary photograph.

| Mite | Where / host context | Where to look and what HiveLens supports |
|---|---|---|
| *Varroa destructor* | Widespread internationally in honey bees | Adult bees and brood cells. Sticky-board counting is the project’s evaluated workflow; record exposure time. Species identity still needs appropriate confirmation. |
| *Varroa jacobsoni* | Associated with Asian honey bees; a host shift to *Apis mellifera* was documented in Papua New Guinea in 2008 | A related Varroa species. HiveLens cannot reliably distinguish it from *V. destructor*. Seek specialist identification. |
| *Tropilaelaps* spp., including *T. mercedesae* and *T. clareae* | Historically associated with Asia; the EU reference laboratory reports spread into the Caucasus | Brood parasites. Not supported by the Varroa detector; follow specialist sampling and local reporting guidance. |
| *Acarapis woodi* | Recorded in multiple beekeeping regions; country status depends on surveillance | A microscopic mite inside adult bee tracheae. Diagnosis requires tracheal examination, not a frame or sticky-board photo. Unsupported by HiveLens. |

A red-brown spot can also be debris. Review the counted and rejected candidates in the
image. An empty count is not proof of an uninfested hive. A sticky-board rate in
**mites/day** is not interchangeable with a wash count in **mites/100 adult bees**.
Keep sampling method, duration, colony and date with every record.

This guide gives no medication doses or universal treatment thresholds. Use local
bee-health advice for sampling, reporting and action, especially for suspected exotic
mites. The application’s status rules are configurable prototype starting points.
